01About us
Who we are

A computer shop that
grew into infrastructure.

CureComp Technology started where most IT businesses do — fixing machines and selling parts in Seremban. The difference is where it went next: managed networks for ministries and manufacturers, a data centre, and Malaysia's first third-party GPON ONT.

What we do, in plain terms

We sell and service computers, parts and supplies. That's the counter you can walk up to at Seremban Plaza.

Behind it, we operate as a Managed Service Provider — supplying computers, laptops, tablets and mobiles, and designing and running networks: PON/GPON, Layer 2 and Layer 3, firewalls, site-to-site VPN and Wi-Fi, with 24/7 live support.

We supply and install networking and Wi-Fi across the whole range, from a home router to ISP-grade equipment, with every roaming access point hardwired back to the switch.

We run cloud and connectivity as a data centre operator, digital infrastructure provider and telecom service provider — dedicated servers, colocation, VM/LXC and IPv6 allocation.

And we are the official supplier and reseller of the Anime4000 GPON Stick and NIJIKA Firmware SDK — the work that put Malaysia's first third-party ONT into customers' hands.

Very few companies cover all of this. That is exactly why nothing gets lost between the hardware vendor, the network team and the host — they're the same people.

05Networking & Wi-Fi
Home · Enterprise · ISP-grade

Networking that behaves like
infrastructure, not a gadget

From one router to a whole exchange — fast when it's busy, which is the only time it matters. Every design is surveyed, cabled, tuned and documented.

Our rule

Hardwired, always.

Every roaming access point is cabled back to the switch — copper with PoE in the building, fibre between blocks. No wireless mesh: each hop halves throughput, latency stacks up, and one weak link degrades everything behind it.

One SSID802.11k / v / r roamingPoE from the rackController you own

FTTR — fibre to the room

When the building won't take Cat6, run fibre instead.

A splitter that behaves like a switch — with no power. Cheaper than a dedicated link per room, mesh-like coverage that is actually wired, and no vendor lock on the access points.

Wi-Fi generations we design for

Wi-Fi 6802.11ax

The repair generation: OFDMA, MU-MIMO, BSS Colouring, Target Wake Time and WPA3.

Wi-Fi 6E6 GHz

The same PHY on clean 6 GHz spectrum — wide channels without legacy clients.

Wi-Fi 7802.11be

MLO, 320 MHz channels, 4096-QAM and Multi-RU — lower jitter under load.

Wi-Fi 8802.11bn

Ultra High Reliability. Tracking the draft; ratification projected for 2028.

Feature project · Thine Technology Sdn. Bhd.

Thousands of phones,
~10 Gbps, one network

Thine Technology runs a remote Android phone platform: physical handsets that overseas users reach the way they would a virtual machine, except every one is a real phone. The platform is theirs. The network under it is ours, built to carry around 10 Gbps and thousands of Wi-Fi 7 clients without drowning in broadcast traffic.

Uplinks

Five links, one gateway

Four TM Unifi 2 Gbps lines terminate on PON Sticks in a MikroTik CRS309, with no ISP boxes, and join a Perfect AS154516 dedicated 10G line on a CCR2116 that load-balances across all five.

Addressing

Layer 3 to every device

Every device is leased its own /32, a subnet of one, so no two devices share a broadcast domain. At 4,000 devices, ARP and broadcast storms have nowhere to form.

Wi-Fi 7

Access points as small cells

APs are planned like LTE/5G cells: capacity comes from AP density, not transmit power, with BSS Colouring and OFDMA keeping hundreds of clients per AP stable.

09Feature project
Thine Technology · designing for density

Wi-Fi that behaves like
a cellular network

Thousands of phones on Wi-Fi look less like an office and more like a cell site. So the design follows a mobile operator's rules (small cells, scheduled airtime, density over power), and we test it with real handsets, not synthetic traffic.

Test method: phone farming
  • Real iPhones and Android handsets as independent clients
  • Continuous background traffic: idle, bursts and roaming
  • Real operating-system behaviour, not synthetic traffic generators
Why it matters
  • Dense Wi-Fi now looks like cellular density
  • Single-client speed tests hide contention problems
  • Shows scheduling, airtime fairness and latency under load
What we observe
  • Wi-Fi 6/7 access points behave like small cells
  • BSS Colouring and OFDMA stabilise dense deployments
  • Performance scales with AP density, not transmit power

Why every device gets a /32

The same devices, addressed two ways.

Flat subnet. One broadcast domain: every ARP request and broadcast reaches every device. At thousands of phones, that noise becomes the traffic.

A /32 per device. Each device is a subnet of one and the router answers for everyone, so broadcasts stop at the device. The network scales by routing, not by flooding.
