Open PON Foundation

CureComp is a member of the Open PON Foundation

The Open PON Foundation exists to keep fibre access open, interoperable and accountable to the people paying for it. As a member, CureComp backs that with product: genuine third-party ONTs, supported firmware, and honest advice about what your operator does and doesn't allow.

It also means the PON Stick isn't a one-off gadget. It sits inside a wider effort to make user-owned optical network terminals normal, documented and supportable — rather than a workaround you're left to figure out alone.

Talk to us about PON

Aligned with a wider movement

The Open PON Foundation shares its vision with the organisations that already proved this model works in adjacent parts of the stack.

Open Networking Foundation ONF — open, software-defined networking and disaggregated access infrastructure, including operator-grade PON.
Electronic Frontier Foundation EFF — the user's right to own, inspect and modify the equipment they paid for, and to keep their connection private.
Linux Foundation Open collaboration and shared, permissively licensed code as the default way infrastructure gets built and maintained.

Named to describe the shared vision. CureComp's membership is of the Open PON Foundation; no endorsement by ONF, EFF or the Linux Foundation is implied.

Why a PON Stick

Stop being the last to know

With an operator-managed ONT, the link is a black box: it either works or you file a ticket and wait. The PON Stick puts the instrumentation on your side of the fibre.

Manage and monitor it yourself

Optical power, temperature, PON state, uptime and alarms — read from your own side and exportable to Prometheus and Grafana, so your SLA numbers are numbers you can actually check.

Peace of mind, not blind trust

No more unattended equipment quietly managed by someone else. You know the state of your own link at any moment, without asking permission.

Recovery in minutes

Swap in a spare stick, re-plug the fibre and you're back. No waiting for a truck roll just to replace a failed box on your wall.

Faster, more stable, lower latency

One less device, one less hop, one less NAT layer between your router and the fibre — a cleaner path with fewer things to go wrong.

Clean division of responsibility

The ISP technician does their part up to the fibre. You simply re-plug it into your router. Troubleshooting stops being an argument.

Less clutter, less power

An SFP-sized module in your router replaces a separate powered box, its adaptor and its patch cable. One device instead of three.

Monitoring

Exports straight into Prometheus and Grafana

The PON Stick doesn't keep its telemetry to itself. Optical levels, PON state and link health can be scraped by Prometheus and rendered in Grafana alongside everything else you already watch — no separate portal, no vendor dashboard you have to remember to check.

That turns a fibre link from something you notice is broken into something you can trend, alert on, and put in front of your ISP with a timestamp attached.
